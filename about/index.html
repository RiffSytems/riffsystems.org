About

Tristan Radford

Cross-Domain Cognitive Systems Architect · Independent researcher

Tristan Radford is a Cross-Domain Cognitive Systems Architect and independent researcher. His work examines how complex systems organise, maintain coherence, adapt, respond to pressure, and acquire or exhibit behaviour that can appear cognitive or meaningful.

His research is deliberately cross-domain. Rather than beginning with assumptions about whether a system is biological, artificial, ecological or planetary, he develops explicit behavioural and causal criteria and then tests how well those criteria survive across very different systems. This has led to work spanning human cognition and behavioural regulation, adaptive systems, artificial representations, planetary atmospheres and forest ecology.

Research approach

A central theme of the research is the separation of claims that are often treated as equivalent: persistence from adaptation, correlation from causal contribution, decodability from interpretation, resilience from retained modification, and behavioural appearance from underlying mechanism. The aim is not to force different systems into a single theory, but to develop methods precise enough to reveal where comparisons are justified and where they break down.

Radford develops both conceptual frameworks and empirical tests. The Riff Systems research programme includes DCCOS, the Adaptive Coherence Framework (ACF), Non-Biological Semantics (NBS), Stress Without Stress, and empirical programmes including Titan T1, Forest F1 and controlled NBS studies.

He works independently through Riff Systems Ltd, using public research, reproducible analysis, prospectively frozen criteria where appropriate, explicit failure conditions, strong conventional nulls, and preservation of negative or incomplete results as part of the scientific record.

Broader research interest

His broader interest is in whether rigorous cross-domain comparison can improve the way we reason about cognition, adaptation, coherence and meaning without assuming in advance which kinds of systems are entitled to those descriptions.
