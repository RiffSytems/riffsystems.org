Research Topic

Adaptation, Resilience & Complex Systems

How can resilience, persistence, ecological memory and history dependence be distinguished from stronger claims of adaptive modification in complex systems?

Short answer. Resilience, recovery, persistence and ecological memory can all change how a system behaves after disturbance, but they do not by themselves establish that the system retained a modified response-generating organisation that causally changed a later response. Riff Systems uses the Adaptive Coherence Framework (ACF) to keep those claims separate and to test where the evidence stops.

Why the distinction matters

In ecology and complex-systems research, prior states and disturbances can leave material or informational legacies that shape later recovery. Established forest-resilience literature uses ecological memory for such persistent legacies. The Riff Systems question is narrower: when does history dependence support the stronger claim that a system has adaptively modified how it will respond?

That distinction is tested rather than assumed. Forest F1 applies one frozen six-gate ACF classifier to six forest systems and finds several superficially adaptation-like signatures—resilience, retained reorganisation, altered later performance and ecological memory—without a completed case yet satisfying the full forest-level adaptive-modification criterion.

Questions this work addresses

  • What is the difference between resilience and adaptation?
  • Does ecological memory demonstrate adaptation?
  • How can history dependence be distinguished from retained adaptive modification?
  • What evidence is needed to claim causal adaptation in a complex system?
  • Can non-biological systems be tested for adaptation without assuming the answer from their substrate?

Context from the wider literature

Ecological-memory research describes how biological and material legacies can shape forest responses to later disturbance and contribute to resilience. That literature provides an important comparison class for the stricter causal distinctions tested in ACF and Forest F1.

External context: Johnstone et al., “Changing disturbance regimes, ecological memory, and forest resilience” (USGS / Frontiers in Ecology and the Environment).
